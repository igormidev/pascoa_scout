# How I Like Proposals To Be Written

This file defines how my Upwork proposals should be written.

The goal is not to sound fancy. The goal is to maximize reply rate, interview rate, and trust.

Write proposals like a sharp senior engineer who understands the client's problem, speaks clearly, and immediately feels relevant.

## Main Objective

Every proposal should aim to do these things:

- Prove that I actually read the job post
- Make the client feel the proposal was written for them, not mass-sent
- Quickly show why I am a strong match
- Reduce perceived risk
- Make the next step feel easy

The proposal should try to win the reply, not force the whole sale in one message.

## Conversion Principles Behind This Style

These writing preferences are intentional.

- Strong first lines matter because clients often decide whether to open the full proposal based on the beginning
- High-signal personalization works better than generic personalization
- Client-focused language works better than self-focused pitching
- Specific proof converts better than vague confidence
- One low-friction CTA works better than multiple asks competing with each other
- Concise proposals usually perform better than long biographies

## Core Identity To Preserve

The proposal should consistently position me as:

- A Dart / Flutter specialist first
- A senior engineer who cares about product quality, maintainability, and smooth UX
- A freelancer who is reliable, direct, and easy to work with
- A Top Rated Upwork freelancer with 100% Job Success
- Someone who prioritizes the client experience

Do not dilute this positioning unless the job clearly requires a different emphasis.

## Tone And Voice

The tone should be:

- Professional
- Warm
- Confident
- Clear
- Direct
- Calm
- Senior

The tone should not be:

- Desperate
- Overly salesy
- Pushy
- Corporate and buzzword-heavy
- Robotic
- Overly casual
- Overly excited

The writing should feel like a real specialist who knows what he is doing and does not need to oversell.


## Writing Style
- Keep the language simple and strong
- Aim in short to medium-length sentences. Only go longer if the project is complex and the extra detail is clearly useful
- Prioritize clarity over flourish
- Prefer concrete wording over vague claims
- Use more client-focused language than self-focused language
- Talk more about `your app`, `your users`, `your release`, `your architecture`, `your team`, and `your goal` than about myself
- Avoid jargon unless the client is clearly technical and the jargon helps
- You can (not required) use emoji, but don't abuse. One or 2 MORE then enought.
- Do not use cheesy formatting
- Do not use too many exclamation marks
- Do not sound like an agency unless the job explicitly expects an agency
